# Task 296 — Add --parallel <N|max> and graph scheduling to /runbook-run

Target: claude

## Goal
Make `/runbook-run` run a runbook as its `Depends on:` graph, per feature
`parallel-runbooks`: a `--parallel <N|max>` flag and the `Parallelism:`
header resolve to one parallelism per run; above one, selection takes every
step whose dependencies are `[x]` up to N, each step runs in its own
worktree per `task-engine`'s `worktree.md`, returns are landed one at a
time, `Needs:` steps are barriers, `[~]` may be committed and a local
worktree is the resume signal. A run at parallelism 1 behaves exactly as
today.

## Acceptance criteria
- ARGUMENTS gains `--parallel <N|max>` — N ≥ 2 or `max`, case-insensitive;
  `--parallel 1` forces a sequential run over a header saying otherwise —
  and three argument errors after the ninth, each naming the problem and
  stopping with nothing run: `--parallel` beside `--inline`; `--parallel`,
  or a header `Parallelism:`, on a runbook whose header carries
  `Sequencing:`, the message naming the line and the two ways out
  (`/pipeline-revise` to carry the constraint into the graph, or a
  sequential run); a header value outside the allowed set. § *The
  execution policy* gains a sibling paragraph resolving the parallelism:
  the flag when passed, else the header, else 1.
- Step 1 resolves the parallelism beside the policy, and when it is above
  one reads `./references/worktree.md`'s home,
  `../task-engine/references/worktree.md`, once.
- Step 3 under a parallelism above one selects every step in list order —
  whatever its id — whose marker is `[ ]`, `[~]` or `[!]` and whose every
  `Depends on:` step is `[x]` (an `Archive:` id counting as `[x]`), while
  fewer than N are in flight, with no bound under `max`; a step in flight is
  neither done nor selectable, and a step depending on it waits for it to
  land. `[P]` keeps its rule. The deadlock test — steps remain, none
  selectable, none `[P]` — is taken only when nothing is in flight. The
  bounds `--from`, `--to`, `--only` apply unchanged; `--steps N` counts
  landed steps.
- Steps 4–8 under a parallelism above one: the worktree is created per
  `worktree.md` before the spawn and the preamble names it as the
  repository root, the contract's `<WORKTREE>` filled with it; several
  spawns may be in flight and each result is awaited exactly as § *Wait*
  demands, no step ticked before its result arrives; a `DONE` is landed
  per `worktree.md` § *The orchestrator lands* — fast-forward only, one
  rebase-again message to the same agent when the base moved, push unless
  `--no-push`, worktree removed and branch safely deleted — and then
  marked, given its `Done:` line and committed as today; a
  `QUESTIONS FOR USER` relays as today while other steps keep landing; the
  `[P]` row is unchanged, the step's worktree removed once its agent has
  left the tree clean. On a sequential run `<WORKTREE>` is the base tree's
  path and nothing else changes.
- A `Needs: agent+human` or `Needs: human` step is a barrier: when it is the
  next selectable step in list order nothing more is spawned, every step
  in flight lands, the step runs in the base tree as today, and spawning
  resumes after its commit.
- COMMIT CADENCE states that under a parallelism above one a landed step's
  commit may capture `[~]` for steps still in flight, one commit per
  landed step staging exactly the two files; ONE RUN PER RUNBOOK states the
  resume signal for such a run as `[RUNNING]` plus a local
  `wt/runbook-<id>-step-<n>` worktree, and keeps the `[~]` signal for a
  sequential run. The Stop-hook reply's subagent-WIP case covers several
  spawns in flight.
- A `[!]` step halts spawning; steps in flight land first; the index is
  then `[FAILED]` with `Failed at:` naming the failed step, and the closing
  report names each in-flight step that landed after the failure.
- The nested-runbook refusal and the depth budget are unchanged; THE DEPTH
  BUDGET gains one sentence: a step running `/task-implement --parallel`
  routes through the relay where its agent cannot spawn, and nothing is
  promised about a relayed child's own parallelism.
- `pipeline-engine`'s `routing.md` row for `/runbook-run` lists the flag,
  the worktrees it writes and the three refusals; `./scripts/check-routing.sh`
  and `./scripts/check-home-paths.sh` pass.
- `runbook-run`'s `version:` is bumped (minor); root `VERSION` is bumped
  (minor) with a matching `CHANGELOG.md` section; `./scripts/check-changelog.sh`
  passes.

## Decisions
- `--inline` beside `--parallel` is refused rather than ignored: the inline
  session is one executor, and a silent fallback to sequential would hide
  the header's promise from the user who launched the run.
- The depth budget for a step running `/task-implement --parallel` is left
  open as the feature document leaves it: the body states the relay as the
  answer where an agent cannot spawn and promises nothing about a relayed
  child's own parallelism. Not settled here.

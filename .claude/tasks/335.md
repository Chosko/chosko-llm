# Task 335 — Read parallel runbooks in /runbook-describe and lint them in /pipeline-check

Target: claude

## Goal
Give the two readers of feature `parallel-runbooks` their line each.
`/runbook-describe` shows a parallel runbook as what it is — the header's
`Parallelism:` line, every `[~]` step marked in flight, and, on a runbook
carrying `Parallelism:`, one line counting the steps with
`Depends on: none`, so the honesty of the graph can be judged without
opening a prompt. `/pipeline-check` gains one probe: a runbook header
carrying both `Parallelism:` and `Sequencing:`, the pair the schema forbids.
No worktree probe, since git across machines makes it noise.

## Acceptance criteria
- `/runbook-describe` prints the `Parallelism:` line with the rest of the
  header when present, says nothing about it when absent, and marks a `[~]`
  step as in flight in its per-step line; on a runbook whose header carries
  `Parallelism:` it prints one line, after the header, counting the steps
  whose `Depends on:` is `none`, and on any other runbook prints no such
  line.
- `pipeline-engine`'s `lint.md` gains one probe, numbered after the last,
  severity ERROR, needing `.claude/RUNBOOKS.md` and each runbook's header:
  it fires when a body's header carries both `Parallelism:` and
  `Sequencing:`, and names `/pipeline-revise` as the fix (carry the
  constraint into the graph and drop the line, or drop `Parallelism:`).
  `/pipeline-check`'s body lists it in the register the catalogue uses, and
  `pipeline-engine`'s `SKILL.md` map row for `lint.md` counts it.
- Both commands' `version:` and `pipeline-engine`'s `version:` are bumped;
  root `VERSION` is bumped (patch) with a matching `CHANGELOG.md` section;
  `./scripts/check-changelog.sh`, `./scripts/check-routing.sh` and
  `./scripts/check-home-paths.sh` pass.

## Decisions
- Settled here, without conflict with the feature document: the edge count
  is printed by `/runbook-describe` only on a runbook carrying
  `Parallelism:` — the one place the honesty of the graph is what the
  reader is judging; on a sequential runbook the count would be noise.
